Open for business: Australia's 2.8 million businesses

Australia has more businesses than ever, about one for every ten people. But behind that total, hundreds of thousands open and close every year, and company failures are at a record high.

By Luke Tan · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · October 2026 · Data: Australian Bureau of Statistics and ASIC

Part 1 · Who they are

Most Australian businesses are a single person

The typical business is not a shop with staff. It is a tradie, a rideshare driver or a family trust holding a rental property. Nearly two in three have no employees, and few small businesses ever grow. (A business here is anything with an ABN that is registered for GST and actively trading.)

Part 2 · Where they are

Seven in ten businesses are in a capital city

That matches where people live: the capitals hold 68% of the population. Sole operators are slightly more city-based than businesses with staff.

A fairer comparison is businesses per person. On that measure, Sydney's City and Inner South leads with 264 per 1,000 residents, because head offices cluster there. The Northern Territory Outback has 38.

Farming regions such as Darling Downs–Maranoa (151 per 1,000) rank above most suburbs. Pick Agriculture or Transport & delivery in the map below to see how differently industries are spread.

Part 3 · Births and deaths

Hundreds of thousands open and close every year

The total has climbed steadily since 2013, hiding huge turnover. COVID support kept closures low in 2020, then a record start-up boom followed in 2021.

Part 4 · Industries

Carers and couriers are rising, farms are shrinking

Since 2019, health care businesses are up 61% as more carers work for themselves alongside the NDIS. Transport is up 37%. Agriculture is the only industry that shrank, as farms merge.

Part 5 · When closing isn't a choice

Insolvencies have hit a record

Pandemic debt relief pushed company insolvencies to their lowest since 1999. When it ended and the tax office resumed chasing debts, failures surged to 14,722 in 2024–25, the most on record.

Australia has never had more businesses, yet in 2024–25 more companies failed than ever before.

Growth comes from people working for themselves in care and delivery, mostly in the outer suburbs. But half of new businesses close within three years. Opening a business is easier than keeping it open.
